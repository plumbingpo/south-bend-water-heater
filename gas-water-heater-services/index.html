Most gas water heaters in South Bend, IN work the same basic way. Gas enters through a control valve that doubles as the thermostat and feeds a burner beneath the tank, and the hot exhaust travels up a flue through the middle of the tank and out through the vent. A thermocouple, or a flame sensor on newer electronic models, tells the valve a flame is present; without that signal, the valve shuts off the gas for safety. The design is dependable, but it has a few predictable weak spots. Thermocouples wear out, pilot tubes and burner ports clog, the flame arrestor screen at the base packs up with lint and dust, control valves fail, and vents lose their draft.

How to Tell Something Is Wrong

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • A vent pipe that has separated, rusted, or left scorch marks near the top of the heater
  • A cold tank and a burner that will not come on
  • Rust-colored hot water or rust streaks on the tank
  • Loud rumbling from the bottom of the tank
  • A pilot that lights, then goes out when you release the knob
  • Showers that go cold sooner than they did a year ago
  • A flame that looks orange or floppy rather than steady and blue
  • A faint gas or rotten-egg smell near the heater (leave and call the gas utility)

Why Hire a Professional for Gas Water Heater Services?

Start with the hazards. Every gas water heater involves fuel piping, an open flame, and exhaust that contains carbon monoxide. A loose flare or threaded joint can leak gas, a vent that is disconnected or poorly sloped can spill exhaust back into the room, and a gas valve installed without a leak test is a risk you cannot see. Then there is the diagnostic side: a pilot that goes out can mean a weak thermocouple, a dirty pilot orifice, a clogged flame arrestor, a draft problem, or a failing gas valve, and replacing them one by one is slow and costly.

Prevention Tips That Actually Help

A little routine care goes a long way toward keeping a water heater running well past its expected lifespan. Here are our recommendations:

  • Check the anode rod every three to five years, since it protects the tank from corrosion
  • Watch for a loose, rusted, or sagging vent pipe above the heater
  • Never store flammable liquids near a gas water heater
  • Vacuum lint and dust from the flame arrestor screen at the base a few times a year
  • Make sure your home has CO alarms that work, especially with gas appliances
  • Set the gas valve near 120 degrees, because hotter water costs more and can scald
  • Flush a few gallons from the drain valve periodically to control sediment
